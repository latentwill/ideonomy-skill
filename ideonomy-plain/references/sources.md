# Sources and attribution

Original source review: 2026-09-08; additional archive coverage reviewed 2026-09-19. Properties and Dimensions remains an upstream reference that was unavailable during the original review. This guide distinguishes source claims from this repository's engineering choices. It is not a transcription or exhaustive survey of the monographs.

| Source | What it supports | Consequence for this adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Patrick Gunkel, [Introduction to Ideonomy](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/intro.html), “What Is Ideonomy?” and “Nature of Science” | Exhaustiveness is an ideal; the proposed laws are tentative. Inquiry includes criticism and validation. | Bound the explored space, label conjectures, and propose checks. |
| Gunkel, [Subdivisions of Ideonomy](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/division.html), foreword | The 235 listed divisions are provisional; their number depends on purpose. | The operational catalog is not a closed canon. Revise classifications when useful. |
| Gunkel, [Dreams for the website](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/dreams.html), newcomer routes | Multiple entry points, navigable scales, and participant-created organons. | Honor user-chosen routes and develop existing artifacts. |
| Grace Kind, [Ideonomy: A Science of Ideas](https://gracekind.net/writing/ideonomy/intro/), “How is ideonomy performed?” | An accessible account of constructing and combining organons. | Retain structured artifacts and credit her synthesis. |
| Kind, [Properties and Dimensions](https://gracekind.net/writing/ideonomy/propertiesanddimensions/) | Properties and substitutions along dimensions. | Use named axes and inspectable transformations. |
| Kind, [Negation in Ideonomy](https://gracekind.net/writing/ideonomy/negation/), “Notating Negation” and democracy example | Property-dependent opposites; illustrative notation and an explicitly invented term. | Distinguish heuristic negation from logical complement and coinage from established usage. |

The [MIT archive](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/legacy-index.html) links the original volumes and charts for deeper work. Its [current introduction](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/) dates Gunkel to 1947–2017 and credits Whitman Richards with hosting and organizing the project. “1937” in the earlier skill was incorrect.

The random tuple mechanism, cooldown, current catalog counts, rendering policies, worked example, and evaluation rubric are contemporary contributions. Method files inherit this distinction unless they identify a particular source. Do not attribute every example or extension to Gunkel or Kind, or treat the archive's historical predictions as established results. Existing repository prose credits and licenses remain in force; links to external works do not relicense those works.

## Archive coverage added in this version

The installed catalog now has picker-ready adaptations for all 24 entries in Gunkel's explicit **“24 Ways to Treat Paths”** organon, while retaining the eight earlier operational adaptations. The primary list appears under “24 WAYS TO TREAT PATHS” in the Blue Volume’s printed section **D-1** (the PDF’s archive search exposes this section label; the static archive does not provide a stable PDF page index): [Blue Volume PDF](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/pdf/Ideonomy_Blue.pdf):

1. clarify, categorize, survey, systematize, or define;
2. compare, analogize, or distinguish;
3. construct;
4. construct theories, hypotheses, or conjectures;
5. criticize;
6. deduce or describe history;
7. discover laws originating or governing the subject;
8. evaluate;
9. exemplify or re-exemplify;
10. experiment or test;
11. explore, examine, or perceive;
12. extrapolate, complete, or supplement;
13. generalize;
14. improve or perfect;
15. measure, quantify, or mathematicize;
16. model, simulate, logicize, or conceptualize;
17. plan research;
18. predict;
19. remake, modify, transform, transelement, or define variations;
20. reuse or use;
21. situate or describe environments, contexts, events, conditions, or circumstances;
22. subdivide or decompose;
23. summarize, simplify, or epitomize;
24. verbally describe, discuss, or explain.

The exact local mapping is: 1 clarify-categorize; 2 compare-analogize; 3 organon-construction; 4 construct-hypotheses; 5 critique; 6 history; 7 law-finding; 8 evaluate; 9 exemplify; 10 experiment-test; 11 explore; 12 extrapolate; 13 generalize; 14 improve; 15 measure; 16 model-simulate; 17 research-plan; 18 predict; 19 transform; 20 reuse; 21 situate; 22 decompose; 23 summarize; 24 explain. These are contemporary procedural adaptations of the source list, not claims that Gunkel specified the exact step sequences used here.

The archive also describes expansion operations and organon families beyond the 24-path treatment list. The [Yellow Volume glossary](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/pdf/glossary_yellow.pdf) (printed glossary p. 76) says that organons can expand through combinations, permutations, transformations, generalizations, specializations, intersections, interactions, reapplications, and recursive use; it also lists physical and virtual forms such as sets, lists, charts, diagrams, maps, ideograms, indexes, files, books, dictionaries, encyclopedias, libraries, globes, questionnaires, scenarios, strategic games, simulations, and programs. The [Bright Green Volume](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/pdf/what_ideonomy_can_do.pdf) (printed p. 164) names further knowledge meta-structures including trees, chains, series, networks, radiations, lattices, matrices, rings, circuitries, fractals, plexures, cellular automata, myrioramas, dualities, and asymmetries. Operational coverage for every named form is in [organon-forms](organon-forms.md): it links each form to a local organon, operator, or explicit composition of them. The [legacy index](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/legacy-index.html) states that the memorial site is static and that its collection and indexing are incomplete, so no universal completeness claim is warranted.

The [Investigable Dimensions essay](https://ideonomy.mit.edu/investigable/inv_text.html) is also relevant to expansion: it explicitly describes dimensions generating further dimensions through combinations, permutations, and transformations, and imagines hierarchies, networks, maps, constellations, concatenations, and user-generated supplementary organons. This supports retaining the existing dimension prompts while treating them as a small contemporary battery rather than an archive-complete list.
