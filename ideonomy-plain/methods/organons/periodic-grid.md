# Organon: periodic-grid

A chart used to investigate missing combinations under an explicit model. The periodic-table analogy is a prompt to look for regularities, not evidence that any arbitrary grid predicts real entities. Contemporary catalog extension; see [source distinctions](../../references/sources.md).

## Procedure

1. Define the domain and two meaningful dimensions with their values. State the rule that makes a missing combination worth investigating, and whether the axes are exhaustive, sampled, correlated, or uncertain.
2. Populate known examples separately from proposed instances. Include support for “known” claims when the task depends on their accuracy.
3. Classify unfilled cells: **not examined**, **unknown**, **incompatible under stated assumptions**, or **candidate hypothesis**. Do not collapse these into a generic invention opportunity.
4. For a candidate, spell out its required properties, the basis for expecting it, and an observation or construction attempt that could count against it. Check whether it already exists before claiming novelty.
5. Reconsider the dimensions if contradictions or duplicates reveal a poor model. Report coverage within this grid, not completeness of the world.

Pair with dimension-identification and substitution. When no defensible regularity supports a prediction, use the grid as a question map.
