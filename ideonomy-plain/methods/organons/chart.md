# Organon: chart

A 2D grid where rows and columns each represent a dimension. Cells contain instances, descriptions, or notes.

## To chart-ify an idea

1. **Pick two dimensions of the idea.** Use any of the dimension-prompts to surface them. The two dimensions should be reasonably independent — charting Naturalness × Synthetic-ness is wasted effort because the axes redundantly say the same thing.
2. **Lay them out as a grid.** Rows are values of one dimension, columns are values of the other.
3. **Populate cells.** For each cell, ask "What is the version of this idea where the row-property AND the column-property both hold?" Fill in an instance, a name, a description, or a `?` if you can't think of one.
4. **Read the asymmetries.** Sparse cells may reflect limited search, correlated axes, incompatibility, or missing instances. These are competing explanations to check, not conclusions from the diagram alone.

## What charts surface

- **Empty cells** are questions with specified properties. Label them unexamined, unknown, incompatible under stated assumptions, or candidate hypotheses.
- **Charts expose relationships** — choosing a second dimension invites an independence check; correlated axes can still be informative when labeled.
- **Asymmetries** are diagnostic: limited coverage can motivate either further search or revision of the dimensional model.

## Pairing

Charts are an output organon for **dimension-identification + substitution** combined: the row dimension and column dimension are both substituting their values; the cells are the population of resulting variants.
