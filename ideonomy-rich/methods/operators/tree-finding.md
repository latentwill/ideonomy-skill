# Tree-finding

Identify the dendritic (branching) structure inside or around the idea. Per Gunkel (cited by Grace), trees are a fundamental ideonomic pattern — they appear in classification, decision-making, memory, information theory, government, and computer programs. Finding the tree exposes both the idea's *context* (what it is part of) and its *decomposition* (what it is made of).

## Procedure

Walk the tree in four directions and record what you find:

1. **Walk up (parents).** What category contains this idea? What contains *that*? Keep going until you reach a root that's too general to be useful. The path up is the chain of supercategories.
2. **Walk down (children).** What are the parts, sub-types, instances, or examples of this idea? Recurse into each. The leaves are the atomic units.
3. **Walk across (siblings).** At the idea's level of generality, what are its peers? These are the alternatives at the same abstraction — frequently the most useful expansion, because siblings share a parent and so share most properties.
4. **Walk by level of generality.** What's between the leaf and the root? Each intermediate level is a candidate framing of the idea. The idea you start with might not be at the most useful level — sliding up or down often reframes the problem.

## Why it works

Trees enforce *exhaustion at a level*: when you list an idea's siblings, you're forced to ask whether your enumeration is complete. The empty branches in a tree are as informative as the full ones — they identify questions about missing examples, impossible categories, or an incomplete classification.

## Pairing

Tree-finding pairs naturally with:

- **organon-construction** — the tree is itself an organon; you're building one as you walk.
- **substitution** — siblings are property-substitutions of each other; finding siblings *is* doing substitution at scale.
- **abstraction-lift** — walking up the tree IS abstraction-lift, made operational.

## Example

For the idea *democracy*:

- Up: form-of-government → political-system → social-coordination-mechanism → cooperation-pattern
- Down: direct-democracy / representative-democracy / liquid-democracy / sortition; each subdivides further
- Across (siblings of *form of government*): monarchy, oligarchy, theocracy, anarchy, technocracy, geniocracy, …
- Levels: at the level of *cooperation patterns*, democracy sits next to markets, juries, festivals, and gift economies — a different and often more interesting peer set than the conventional one.
